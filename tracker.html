RFP Evaluation Framework — score any opportunity across 6 strategic dimensions (click to expand)

Strategic Leverage vs. Commodity Work: Is this RFP an opportunity to solve a complex gap that positions us as a long-term partner, or is it a commodity request where the only differentiator will be budget/cost? Use these six dimensions to evaluate any RFP before committing resources.

1 Supporter Need (The Problem Behind the Ask)

Supporter signal: What is changing in the market or supporter priority?

  • What unmet educational need is the supporter signaling? Is this need scientific, clinical, behavioral, operational, or outcomes-driven?
  • Why does this topic matter to them right now? Are they responding to a recent drug approval, an upcoming Phase 3 trial catalyst, new clinical guideline updates, or competitive pressure?
  • What is the underlying business or educational problem behind the request? Are they trying to raise baseline awareness, drive practice adoption, reach a new audience (e.g., community PCPs or APPs), or prepare the market for launch?

2 Learner Gap (The True Clinical Barrier)

What is the learner not doing, not understanding, or not applying in practice?

  • Is the barrier a lack of disease state awareness, or is it pathway confusion, lack of confidence, or missing clinical workflows?
  • What level of learning is required? Is this an awareness gap (requires foundational text/slides), a decision-making gap (requires case-based learning/simulations), or an implementation gap (requires tools, workflows, or Quality Improvement)?
  • What specific clinical decision point is causing hesitation or inertia? Where are HCPs getting stuck when selecting, sequencing, or escalating therapies?

3 Capability & Program Design (Matching Format to Gap)

Does the requested format actually solve the underlying learner gap?

  • If the RFP asks for a standard webinar, but the gap is real-world decision-making, can we propose case-based simulations or interactive polling to drive application?
  • Who needs this education most, and why now? If targeting community providers, do they need practical, accessible tools rather than high-level academic theory?
  • Is this a single-touch activity or a multi-touchpoint strategy? Does the gap require predisposing, enabling, and reinforcing elements across the learning continuum?

4 Strategic Differentiator & Value Proposition

What will make our response consultative instead of just compliant?

  • How do we demonstrate that we understand their strategic intent better than competitors?
  • Which capability creates the strongest value? Can we leverage AI-native patient simulations (ACTiconsult), precision community targeting (Activeer), or existing content assets to maximize impact?
  • How will we prove impact ("So what? Who cares?")? How will our outcomes plan measure movement in competence, confidence calibration, or intended practice change rather than basic attendance?

5 Budget, Feasibility & Constraints

What are the hard non-negotiables?

  • What are the submission deadlines, grant portal rules, word limits, accreditation requirements, and budget caps?
  • Is the concept right-sized for the budget reality? If the budget is tight, what deliverables must stay to preserve core value, and what can be streamlined or built using content leverage?
  • What's the opportunity cost — what are we NOT pursuing by committing here?

6 Pipeline & Long-Term Opportunity

Could this lead to a follow-up concept or multi-support opportunity?

  • If a remaining gap is anticipated after this activity, how can we position the outcomes to support future educational initiatives?
  • Is the company's pipeline deep enough to sustain a multi-year franchise?
  • Does winning this open doors to adjacent therapeutic areas or larger grants?
  • Does it position us in a space where competitors haven't established presence?

Strategic-leverage questions — ask before every bid

  • Strategic leverage vs. commodity work: Is this RFP an opportunity to solve a complex gap that positions us as a long-term partner, or is it a commodity request where the only differentiator will be budget/cost?
  • Risk tolerance: Does the supporter genuinely prioritize innovation (willingness to test a new format or pathway), or are they looking for certainty (a proven format we have executed successfully for them or others before)?
  • Relationship trajectory: Does winning this proposal create a natural "next step" or pathway for future funding, or does it exist in a silo that leaves us at square one for the next fiscal cycle?
  • Feasibility vs. ambition: Given the stated constraints (budget and timeline), can we deliver measurable impact without over-promising or compromising the quality of the outcomes data we need to collect?
  • Stakeholder alignment: Beyond the immediate contact, have we identified the "silent" decision-maker (e.g., the person who cares about metrics vs. the person who cares about clinical reputation), and does our proposed solution solve their specific pain point?

Vera Therapeutics · nephrology · IgA nephropathy (TRUTAKNA / atacicept)

Cost of inaction

TRUTAKNA is the first BAFF/APRIL dual inhibitor to reach commercial launch (accelerated approval 7 Jul 2026). Without sequencing and mechanism-of-action education seeded now, community nephrologists will default to whatever molecule their local sales rep detailed first — driving inappropriate combination use with existing SGLT2i / ERAs / endothelin blockers and preventable adverse-event signals in the first 12 months post-launch.

Differentiation — what Decera Clinical could uniquely do

Medscape and PeerView are running mechanism-primer IgAN activities funded by the incumbent (sparsentan/Filspari) and Novartis (Fabhalta). None currently offer a supporter-neutral, five-mechanism comparative-sequencing curriculum built around the NEJM ORIGIN-3 evidence base. Decera Clinical could uniquely deliver a case-based sequencing framework with a structured post-activity outcomes assessment tied to guideline-adherent prescribing patterns.

Supporter perspective — why now

Vera stood up its Medical Affairs org this quarter and has NOT yet published a grants portal — first-mover education partners set the reference terms. Their commercial priority through 2027 is establishing TRUTAKNA share of voice against Novartis Fabhalta and pre-empting Otsuka's sibeprenlimab launch; education positioning atacicept in the dual BAFF/APRIL class is directly aligned with that.

Source: Vera Therapeutics — Positive ORIGIN Phase 3 Data (NEJM, ASN Kidney Week 2025)

Otsuka · nephrology · IgA nephropathy (sibeprenlimab / anti-APRIL)

Cost of inaction

Sibeprenlimab VISIONARY interim data (NEJM Nov 2025) show statistically and clinically meaningful proteinuria reduction. When approved, IgAN will have five approved mechanisms with zero head-to-head data. Without comparative-mechanism education pre-launch, community nephrologists will make sequencing decisions on payer coverage alone; academic centers will over-select CAR-T-adjacent B-cell depleting strategies for patients whose IgAN could be managed with sequential targeted therapy.

Differentiation — what Decera Clinical could uniquely do

Clinical Care Options currently runs an anti-APRIL–focused activity in the nephrology space (funded by a competing sponsor). What is missing: a nurse-practitioner and PA curriculum for community nephrology sites, where 65% of IgAN patients are managed. Decera Clinical could uniquely deliver a mid-level-directed IgAN sequencing curriculum — a format Otsuka's cardio/renal team has funded in HFpEF but not yet in nephrology.

Supporter perspective — why now

Otsuka is an established renal funder (Jynarque/Samsca legacy); this is submit-ready, not warm-up. FDA action anticipated 2026-27; their US medical affairs team is scoping pre-launch grants NOW for activities landing in the 6-month post-approval window.

Source: Otsuka US — Sibeprenlimab Phase 3 Data (VISIONARY, NEJM 2025)

Travere · nephrology · IgAN and FSGS (sparsentan / FILSPARI)

Cost of inaction

Sparsentan received full FSGS approval Apr 2026 — the first-ever pharmacotherapy for a disease with zero prior approved options. FSGS prescriber inertia is severe (nephrologists default to immunosuppression + supportive care learned in fellowship). Without practical initiation and monitoring education, sparsentan uptake in FSGS will underperform payer models and Travere will pull grant funding by 2027.

Differentiation — what Decera Clinical could uniquely do

NKF runs the benchmark IgAN Clinical Education Series (Travere-funded). No provider is currently offering an FSGS-specific enduring curriculum with case-based ERA initiation protocols, LFT monitoring, and REMS practice patterns. Decera Clinical could uniquely deliver a REMS-integrated FSGS initiation toolkit — the format Travere has said they need but haven't been offered.

Supporter perspective — why now

Travere has the most mature grants office in the emerging-supporter set (CyberGrants portal live). Q4 2026 planning cycle for 2027 grants is open now. Their strategic priority is FSGS pull-through — not incremental IgAN activities, which are already saturated.

Source: Travere Therapeutics — Medical Education Grants portal

Apellis · nephrology · C3G and primary IC-MPGN (EMPAVELI / pegcetacoplan)

Cost of inaction

EMPAVELI became the FIRST and ONLY approved therapy for C3G and primary IC-MPGN in Jul 2025 (patients aged 12+). Prescriber familiarity is at floor: many nephrologists have never diagnosed a C3G/MPGN case and rely on biopsy pathologist interpretation. Without diagnostic-pathway and complement-cascade education, patients will remain undiagnosed for 6-24 months post-biopsy, and post-transplant recurrence — the highest-morbidity subgroup — will be missed entirely.

Differentiation — what Decera Clinical could uniquely do

No provider currently offers an integrated nephrologist + renal-pathologist joint curriculum for C3G/IC-MPGN. Existing complement-cascade CME (from Alexion/AstraZeneca-supported providers) is PNH-anchored, not glomerular. Decera Clinical could uniquely deliver a diagnostic-first curriculum co-developed with a renal-pathology society partner (ASN Renal Pathology Society or Arkana Labs registry) — a structural gap Apellis has flagged publicly.

Supporter perspective — why now

Apellis + Sobi have a joint commercial launch to fund; the education window is 12-18 months post-approval before payer coverage ossifies. Their Q3 2026 grant docket is open; Sobi's ex-US expansion adds international CME priorities uncommon at emerging supporters.

Source: Apellis — FDA Approves EMPAVELI as First C3G and Primary IC-MPGN Treatment
